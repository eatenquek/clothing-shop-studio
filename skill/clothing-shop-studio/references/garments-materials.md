# Garments and materials

Use this reference to ask precise questions and to explain trade-offs. Weight ranges are typical market guidance, not specifications; the producer's fabric data is authoritative. Never quote prices.

## Garment families

| Family | Common subtypes | Ask about |
|---|---|---|
| Tee | crew, V-neck, pocket, oversized/boxy, cropped, ringer | fit, length, neck rib width, side-seamed or tubular |
| Long-sleeve | crew long-sleeve, raglan, mock neck | cuff finish (rib or hemmed), sleeve length, body length |
| Tank | muscle, racerback, singlet | armhole depth, strap width, drop |
| Performance top | running tee, training tee, singlet, base layer | knit, wicking, ventilation, stretch, flatlock seams, reflective details |
| Polo | piqué, performance polo | collar type, placket length, buttons |
| Sweatshirt / hoodie | crewneck, pullover hoodie, zip hoodie, quarter-zip | fleece type, hood lining, pocket, zip, drawcords |
| Jacket | coach, windbreaker, anorak, bomber, varsity, puffer, chore coat, softshell | shell fabric, lining, insulation, closures, weather treatment |
| Bottoms | sweatpants, joggers, shorts, cargo, leggings | rise, inseam, waistband, pockets, hem |
| Headwear | dad cap, structured cap, trucker, five-panel, beanie, bucket hat | crown profile, panels, closure, front seam, decoration area |
| Bag / accessory | tote, duffel, waist bag, socks, bandana, patch, label | construction, handle or strap, knit or print method |

Identify the family first, then the subtype. Headwear, bags, and socks rarely need GSM; ask about their specific construction instead.

## Fit

Fit terms: slim, regular, relaxed, oversized/boxy, cropped. Oversized fits usually combine a dropped shoulder, wider body, and shorter relative length. Fit is visual, so show four options when the user is unsure. Record whether the size range is unisex, men's, women's, or youth; unisex blocks run larger on smaller wearers.

## Construction

Ask only what matters for the garment: side-seamed versus tubular bodies, set-in versus raglan versus drop-shoulder sleeves, rib versus self-fabric necklines and cuffs, flatlock seams for performance wear, taped necks, double-needle hems, pocket types, closures, linings, and seam placement relative to planned artwork (a centre-front seam on a cap, a side seam under a wrap print).

## Fibres

- **Cotton**: breathable and soft; combed or ring-spun is smoother and prints more crisply. Shrinks unless pre-shrunk. Ideal for screen printing and DTG.
- **Polyester**: quick-drying, colourfast, and required for sublimation. Traps odour more readily; plastisol can migrate dye on some dyed polyester without a low-bleed ink.
- **Blends (CVC, 50/50, tri-blend)**: softer drape and less shrinkage; prints look slightly vintage because the synthetic fibres take ink differently.
- **Nylon**: strong and smooth, used for shells; often coated or DWR-finished, which limits decoration.
- **Elastane / spandex**: adds stretch and recovery; heat-sensitive.
- **Wool / merino**: temperature-regulating; needs gentle care.

## Structures

Knits stretch and suit tees, sweatshirts, and activewear: single jersey (tees), interlock (smooth, stable), piqué (polos, textured), rib (cuffs, fitted tops), waffle (thermal texture), French terry (loops inside), brushed fleece (napped, warmer), and mesh (ventilation). Wovens are stable and suit shirts, bottoms, and outerwear: poplin, twill, canvas or duck, denim, ripstop, and taffeta shells.

## Fabric weight

Typical ranges in GSM:

| Garment | Light | Mid | Heavy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tee / long-sleeve | 140–160 | 170–200 | 220–300 |
| Performance top | 100–130 | 130–160 | 160+ |
| Sweatshirt / hoodie | 240–280 | 300–350 | 380–450 |

Heavier fabric feels premium, holds structure, and resists see-through; lighter fabric drapes and breathes. Heavyweight tees suit oversized streetwear silhouettes.

## Climate and weight

In hot, humid climates such as Singapore, heavy cotton long-sleeves and fleece trap heat and dry slowly. When a user asks for a heavy garment for a humid climate, explain the trade-off once and let them choose: keep the weight for structure and look, move to a mid-weight (about 180–200 GSM) with a boxy cut, choose a more open knit or a garment-washed finish, or accept seasonal or indoor wear. Record the decision; do not silently change their request.

## Hand-feel

Describe hand-feel in the user's words: soft, dry, crisp, brushed, peached, slubby, smooth, structured. Finishes change it: enzyme or garment washing softens and adds a lived-in look; brushing adds warmth; silicone softeners feel slick and can affect print adhesion.

## Performance

Record stretch (none, mechanical, two-way, four-way), moisture management (wicking finish or fibre), ventilation (mesh panels, perforation), opacity (light colours and thin knits can show through; ask how important coverage is), UV protection, and anti-odour treatments. Performance claims such as UPF ratings need supplier test data; do not state them as facts.

## Care

Ask for care expectations when they affect fabric or decoration choices: machine wash temperature, tumble drying, and ironing. Cold washing preserves prints and reduces shrinkage; plastisol prints must not be ironed directly.
